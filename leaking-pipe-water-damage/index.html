Leaking Pipe Water Damage in Trenton: What to Know

A leaking pipe rarely announces itself. Water seeps out behind drywall, at the back of a sink cabinet or under the concrete until a stain, a soft floor or an unexplained water bill gives it away. By then the materials around the pipe have often been wet for weeks. Our team in Trenton finds how far the moisture has spread and dries all of it, not just the part you can see.

The plumbing repair stops the water, but it does not remove what has already soaked in. Getting it out is the work we do. Our crew maps the damp materials with thermal imaging and moisture meters, opens up no more than necessary, and runs dehumidifiers and air movers until the readings return to normal. A slow leak gives mold plenty of time to begin, so we inspect for growth too and treat any surface it has reached.

Do This First

Your First Steps

  1. Stop the Water SupplyFor a leak at a toilet or sink, turn off the small supply valve feeding that fixture. If you cannot find the source, turn off the main shutoff valve for the house.
  2. Keep Power AwayIf water is near outlets, switches or a light fixture, switch off the breaker for that area. Keep your hands off cords and devices that have gotten wet.
  3. Check the Water MeterMake sure nothing in the house is using water and read the meter. If the dial is still moving, a pipe is leaking somewhere you cannot see, possibly under the slab.
  4. Empty and PhotographClear out the cabinet or closet near the leak and take photos of stains, warped materials and the pipe itself before anything is repaired or thrown away.
  5. Have the Area MeteredBefore the opening is sealed, get the surrounding materials checked with a moisture meter. Inside the cavity, moisture can remain even when the wall surface feels dry.
Common Causes

How It Usually Starts

  • A hairline split left by freezing weather in a line running through a crawl space or outside wall.
  • A nail or screw driven into a pipe during a remodel or while hanging something on the wall.
  • A P-trap or drain connection under a sink that loosens and drips each time the basin empties.
  • Water pressure set too high, which wears on every joint and valve until a slow weep begins.
  • Supply connections and compression fittings behind toilets or under sinks that have worn out or worked loose.
If It Is Left

Where the Damage Spreads

Ceiling and Drywall Damage

Leaks over a ceiling or behind a wall surface show as bubbling paint and brown stains. If the board keeps taking on water it loses its stiffness, starts to sag and may have to be removed.

Swollen Cabinets

Particleboard cabinet bases under a sink swell and crumble once they absorb water. Swollen board stays swollen, and after a lengthy leak the base often has to be replaced.

Flooring Damage

Water from a slab or wall leak travels under flooring. Hardwood cups, laminate swells at the seams, and adhesive under vinyl and tile lets go.

Hidden Mold Growth

Nothing suits mold better than the dark, moist space inside a wall. It can spread across the hidden face of the drywall long before anything appears on the room side, which is common when a leak is slow.

Will Insurance Pay?

What About Insurance?

Insurers draw a line between a sudden leak and a slow one. Sudden and accidental damage is often covered, but damage from a leak that went on for weeks or months usually is not, and some policies exclude it by name. A hidden leak you had no way of knowing about may be handled more favorably than one that was visible and ignored. Check your policy and ask your agent. So the adjuster can work from solid facts, we provide photos, moisture readings and notes on the leak source.

Cost

What Drives the Price

  • What has to be torn out and put back, from drywall and insulation to cabinets and flooring.
  • Whether the leak lasted days or months, and the size of the area it wet.
  • Mold, if there is any, because containment and removal come first and drying finishes after.
  • Where the leak is, since an open cabinet costs less than a wall or slab.

How We Fix It

How We Handle Leaking Pipe Water Damage

1

Find Every Wet Area

We scan walls, floors and cabinets with moisture meters and a thermal camera to mark every wet area, including spots well away from the pipe where water has traveled along framing.

2

Open What Is Needed

We make targeted openings to reach wet cavities, remove drywall, insulation or cabinet parts that cannot be saved, and bag any moldy material so spores are not spread through the house.

3

Structural Drying

We keep dehumidifiers and air movers going until the subfloor and framing read normal, which usually takes 3 to 5 days. On every visit we take new readings and reposition equipment as things dry out.

4

Final Treatment and Records

We apply antimicrobial treatment to affected surfaces and give you moisture logs and photos. Those records show the area was dry before repairs and help with any claim.
