Attic Water Damage: What Trenton Homeowners Should Know

There are two ways an attic ends up wet. Worn out shingles, flashing that has failed, and the seals around chimneys and vents all let rain in from overhead. From below, household moisture rises and condenses on the cold roof sheathing if attic ventilation is poor. Either way, hardly anyone goes up there to look. For Trenton homeowners, our crew examines the attic, identifies where the water comes from, and dries the space.

On the typical attic job, soaked insulation is the main issue. Once wet, both cellulose and fiberglass lose R-value, and heating and cooling the home costs more. Even worse, it keeps water pressed against the framing and the ceiling drywall for weeks. Saturated material comes out, air movers and dehumidifiers dry the wood, and any mold on the sheathing or rafters gets treated.

Signs to Look For

  • A patch of insulation that is flatter, more matted, or darker than the rest
  • Spots of daylight at a chimney, a vent pipe, or a valley in the roof
  • Ceilings upstairs with brownish stains or rings that grow following storms
  • An upstairs ceiling where the paint has blistered or started to peel
  • Dark stains or streaks on the rafters and the underside of the roof deck
  • Rusty nail tips poking through the sheathing, sometimes with water beads on them
Our Process

Our Attic Water Damage Process

1

Inspect and Trace

Our inspection covers the flashing points, roof deck, exhaust ducts and vents, and tells us if the water is condensation, a leak, or both. Thermal imaging and moisture meters show how far it reached.

2

Remove Wet Insulation

Saturated or moldy insulation is bagged and removed so the wood and drywall under it can dry. We protect the living space below and keep the access path contained.

3

Dry the Framing

With dehumidifiers and air movers, the sheathing, joists and rafters return to normal moisture levels. An antimicrobial goes on after any visible mold has been cleaned from the wood.

4

Correct the Source

You are shown the ventilation or roof problem to be corrected, and our findings can go to your roofer. Once the attic stays dry, new insulation goes in to restore the R-value.

If Nothing Is Done

The Damage Timeline

First storm

Insulation Takes It

The insulation catches and absorbs whatever rain gets by the roofing. Because the rooms below show nothing, the leak is missed and no roof repair happens.

Hours 24 to 48

Mold May Begin

In a still, warm attic, paper facing and wood that stay damp give mold ideal conditions. Growth can begin on the sheathing and on the back of the ceiling drywall.

Weeks later

Stains Come Through

Once storm after storm has saturated the insulation, the ceiling starts getting wet. Brown rings appear in the room below, and the drywall may start to sag.

Months later

Wood Starts Rotting

The sheathing comes apart in layers and no longer grips the roofing nails. Mold spreads across large sections of the roof deck, and the rafters remain damp enough to decay.

What You Cannot See

Risks That Are Easy to Miss

Mold on Sheathing

Poorly ventilated attics often grow mold across the underside of the sheathing. Before anyone looks, it may already have spread over a large section.

Ceiling Collapse

Insulation full of water weighs a lot. The ceiling drywall under it weakens where it is fastened, and it can give way suddenly and bring the wet insulation along.

Lost Insulation Value

Insulation that is damp and compressed barely insulates, and drying may not bring it back. The rooms beneath that section get colder or hotter, and you pay more for energy.

Bath Fans Venting Inside

Some bathroom fans exhaust into the attic and never reach the outdoors, adding warm, damp air daily. That moisture condenses on the cold sheathing and looks just like a roof leak.
