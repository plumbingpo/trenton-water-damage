Water Heater Leak Cleanup in Trenton: What to Know

Even though the heater itself contains 40 to 80 gallons, the leak is not capped at that amount. Your supply line keeps feeding the tank, which means it fills back up just as quickly as water escapes through a rusted bottom. Until someone closes the cold water valve, it keeps running. How bad it gets depends on placement, whether that is the garage, a closet in the hall or an attic sitting above finished rooms, which is the worst.

Our Trenton team extracts the water, then looks at what surrounded the heater. The platform beneath the tank and the base of the closet walls are usually soaked. In a garage, water reaches the wall shared with the house and anything stored on the floor. Water from an attic unit soaks through insulation, then through the ceiling. Our crew takes readings in every area and sets drying equipment to bring it back to normal.

First Five Minutes

Your First Steps

  1. Turn Off the Heat SourceFor an electric heater, switch off its breaker. With a gas heater, the knob on the control goes to the off position. Never leave a heating element or burner running on an empty tank.
  2. Shut the Cold Water ValveFind the cold water shutoff above the heater and turn it clockwise. If it is stuck or leaking, shut off the main water valve for the house.
  3. Beware of Scalding WaterWater from the tank can scald. Keep children and pets away, and do not wade through pooled water until it has had time to cool.
  4. Empty the Tank OutsideA garden hose on the drain valve, if you can get to it safely, lets you send what is left in the tank outside or into a floor drain instead of across the floor.
  5. Move Stored ItemsGet rugs, boxes and other absorbent things up off the wet floor. Cardboard and fabric soak up water quickly and hold it against walls and concrete.
Will Insurance Pay?

What About Insurance?

A sudden tank failure is often treated as accidental and sudden, so the resulting damage to belongings, floors and walls is often covered. Usually the unit is left out of the claim, as age related wear is viewed as a maintenance matter. If an adjuster finds signs of a long-term leak, such as rust trails or old staining, the claim can be reduced or denied because gradual leaks usually are not covered. Check your policy, photograph the heater and the damage, and keep the old unit until the claim is settled.

Why It Happens

What Causes It

  • A temperature and pressure relief valve that opens or fails and discharges water.
  • Drain pans beneath closet or attic units that rust through or clog and then spill over.
  • Corroded inlet and outlet fittings or flex connectors on top of the heater.
  • Anode rods that are used up, leaving the steel inside the tank open to rust.
  • A missing expansion tank or a failed thermostat that lets too much pressure build inside.
What Is at Risk

What the Water Damages

Flooring in Nearby Rooms

Carpet, hardwood or laminate in the hallway gets wet as water escapes below the closet door. Because the water is hot, wood floors swell faster and the backing on carpet can let go.

Stored Belongings

Whatever is kept by a closet or garage heater, from keepsakes to furniture and boxes, is the first thing to get wet. Mold can develop on damp contents that are not promptly dried or tossed, while cardboard boxes disintegrate.

Attic and Ceilings

A heater in the attic sends water through insulation onto the ceiling below. Wet insulation stops insulating and its weight can bring ceiling drywall down.

Shared Garage Wall

The wall between the garage and the house takes water into its bottom plate and drywall from the slab, and from there it reaches the floors of the living space.

Our Process

How We Handle Water Heater Leak Cleanup

1

Remove Standing Water

Standing water is taken up from the garage, closet or floor. With an attic unit we also clear the ceiling cavity and overflow pan, which keeps nearby materials from soaking up more.

2

Check Around the Heater

To learn the full reach of the water, our crew meters and scans the platform, the bottoms of the walls, neighboring rooms and the ceiling below if there is one.

3

Remove Wet Material

Soaked insulation, crumbling drywall and damaged baseboards are taken out. In attics we clear wet insulation so the ceiling joists and drywall below can be dried.

4

Dry and Monitor

Air movers and dehumidifiers run for the 3 to 5 days drying usually takes. We record moisture readings and tell you when the space is ready for the new heater.
